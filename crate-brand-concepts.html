Crate

A personal music archiving tool, styled after the physicality of a record crate — pressed labels, stacked sleeves, worn grooves — carried into a modern desktop app. Three directions below, all built from that one idea rather than a generic app-icon default.

Ink and Vellum carry the app's two surfaces (dark shell, light content areas). Wax is the one accent that gets to shout — status highlights, the wordmark. Oxblood is structural, not decorative: it's the "second sleeve" behind every panel here. Signal is reserved entirely for live states — downloading, syncing — so it never competes with Wax.

Direction A — Spindle

16px tray size

The record label, minimal

A pressed vinyl label rendered as a flat mark: spindle hole standing in for the record itself, wordmark following the label's own curve instead of sitting straight.

Grounded directly in the object a crate holds. Reads instantly as "music" without a note/headphone cliché, and the spindle hole alone (no wordmark) still works as a 16px tray icon.

Direction B — Leaning Stack

16px tray size

Records leaning in the crate

Three sleeves tipped against each other, the way records actually sit when a crate isn't full — the center one upright and gold marks the app itself among the collection.

Most literal to "Crate" without drawing an actual crate (which reads dated/skeuomorphic). The asymmetric lean keeps it from settling into a generic three-bar logo.

Direction C — Groove Arc

16px tray size

Grooves opening into a "C"

Concentric record grooves, broken open on the right side so the negative space reads as the letter C — three arcs, three colors, tightening toward center.

The most "artistic/different" of the three — least literal, most abstract — and it's the only mark built to animate naturally later (grooves could rotate slightly on a sync/download state) without redesigning it.
